What is it

What StakeStone staking means

StakeStone staking means depositing ETH into StakeStone, an omnichain liquid staking and yield protocol, and receiving STONE in return. STONE is a yield-bearing ETH token you hold in your own wallet.

How STONE accrues value

Your STONE balance does not rebase. The number of tokens you hold stays the same, while the ETH value of each STONE is designed to grow over time as the protocol earns yield. Returns vary and are never guaranteed.

Where it can be used

STONE can be bridged to supported networks and used in DeFi, for example as collateral or in liquidity pools, where those venues accept it. StakeStone also offers a BTC version of its product, which follows the same general idea for Bitcoin-based assets.

STONE vs LST

STONE compared with a rebasing LST

Both are liquid ways to hold staked or yield-earning ETH. They differ mainly in how the yield shows up in your wallet.

How to stake

How to deposit into StakeStone

Before you start

You need ETH in a wallet you control, plus a little extra ETH to pay gas. Review the quote shown in the interface before you sign anything.

Step by step

  1. Connect your wallet and choose ETH as the asset to deposit.
  2. Enter an amount and review the expected STONE output and estimated gas.
  3. Sign the deposit transaction in your wallet.
  4. Wait for confirmation; STONE then appears in the same wallet.
  5. To exit, request a withdrawal and wait for it to be processed in a round, or sell STONE on a DEX at the market price.

Withdrawals are handled in rounds or batches rather than instantly, so timing is not fixed. A DEX sale is faster but may fill below the underlying value.

Fees & gas

What StakeStone staking costs

Gas

Depositing, approving and withdrawing are Ethereum transactions paid in native ETH. See how gas works. Bridging STONE to another network adds its own transaction costs.

Protocol fees

StakeStone fees are taken out of the yield rather than charged as a separate line when you deposit. That means they show up as a lower return, not as an extra payment. Check the protocol's own documentation for current terms.

Market cost

If you sell STONE on a DEX instead of using a withdrawal round, the quote includes price impact, slippage and any pool fee. Keep some ETH in your wallet for gas, since holding STONE cannot pay for it.

Security

Risks and staying safe

Non-custodial by design

You connect your own wallet and sign every transaction yourself. This interface never holds your funds or keys, and it is not an official StakeStone site.

Risks to understand

  • Strategy risk: the way the protocol earns yield can lose value or underperform.
  • Smart-contract risk: contracts can contain bugs or be exploited, so risk is never zero.
  • Depeg risk: STONE can trade below its underlying value on a DEX, especially under stress.
  • Bridge risk: moving STONE across networks relies on bridge contracts that can fail.
  • Withdrawal timing: exits via rounds are not instant.

Before you sign

Verify the STONE contract address on each network, check the quote, and only deposit what you can afford to lose.

FAQ

StakeStone Staking FAQ

What is StakeStone staking?

It means depositing ETH into StakeStone and receiving STONE, a yield-bearing token. The value of each STONE is designed to grow over time rather than your balance increasing. Returns vary and are not guaranteed.

Does my STONE balance increase over time?

No, STONE does not rebase, so the number of tokens in your wallet stays the same. The yield is meant to show up as a higher ETH value per token. How an app displays that value can differ.

Can I withdraw immediately?

Not through the protocol. Withdrawals are processed in rounds or batches, so you wait for a round to complete. Alternatively you can sell STONE on a DEX at the market price, which may differ from the underlying value.

Can I use STONE outside Ethereum?

STONE can be bridged to supported networks and used in DeFi where it is accepted. Bridging adds gas and bridge risk, so check which networks and apps support it first.

Is there a BTC option?

StakeStone has a BTC version of its product as well. This interface focuses on depositing ETH for STONE, so check the protocol's own documentation for the BTC side.

How are fees charged?

Fees come out of the yield, so you do not pay a separate charge on deposit. You still pay network gas in ETH, plus any DEX or bridge costs if you use them.
